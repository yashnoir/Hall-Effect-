THE HALL EFFECT // PHYSICS FOUNDATION

Discovered in 1879 by Edwin H. Hall, the Hall Effect occurs when an electric current carries charges through a magnetic field perpendicular to the flow. The moving charge carriers experience a transverse Lorentz force:

FL = q (vd × B)

This transverse force deflects charge carriers to one face of the conductor or semiconductor until an opposing electric field (Hall field EH) builds up to balance the magnetic force. The resulting potential difference is the Hall Voltage (VH).

Why the Sign of RH Classifies the Semiconductor:

  • N-Type Semiconductors (Electrons): Negative charges (q = -e) moving in the opposite direction of conventional current are deflected to the same physical side as positive charges, producing a negative Hall voltage ($V_H < 0$) and a negative Hall coefficient ($R_H < 0$).
  • P-Type Semiconductors (Holes): Positive charge carriers (q = +e) moving in the direction of conventional current produce a positive Hall voltage ($V_H > 0$) and a positive Hall coefficient ($R_H > 0$).
  • Carrier Density: Once $R_H$ is known, carrier concentration is determined via $n = \frac{1}{|R_H| \cdot e}$.
